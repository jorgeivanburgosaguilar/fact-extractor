Nordwind Energie announced on 14 March 2024 that it will build a 480 MW offshore wind farm in the German Bight, roughly 90 kilometres north-west of Helgoland. The project, named Seeadler, is expected to cost €2.1 billion and to begin delivering power to the grid in late 2028.

"This is the largest single investment in our company's forty-year history," said chief executive Ingrid Halvorsen at a press conference in Hamburg. "We are doing it because the permitting reform of 2023 finally made projects at this depth economically viable."

The wind farm will use 60 turbines of 8 MW each, supplied by the Danish manufacturer Vestas under a contract signed in January. Nordwind Energie, founded in 1984 in Bremerhaven, employs 3,200 people and reported revenue of €1.4 billion in 2023.

Not everyone welcomed the plan. The Federal Agency for Nature Conservation warned that the site overlaps a migratory corridor used by common terns, and asked for construction to pause during the spring migration. Halvorsen said the company would comply.

Under German law, offshore wind farms in the exclusive economic zone require approval from the Federal Maritime and Hydrographic Agency, which has scheduled its decision for September 2024. If approval is granted, seabed surveys will start immediately afterwards.
