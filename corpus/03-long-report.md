# Annual Review: Coastal Sediment Monitoring Programme, 2023

## Programme overview

The Coastal Sediment Monitoring Programme was established in 2009 by the Marine Research Council to track erosion and deposition along 340 kilometres of the eastern seaboard. It is funded jointly by the Council and the Department of Infrastructure, with a 2023 operating budget of £8.7 million. The programme is directed by Dr. Amara Okonjo, who succeeded Dr. Peter Lindqvist in April 2022.

The programme maintains 74 permanent monitoring stations. Each station records beach profile elevation, grain size distribution, and nearshore wave height at two-week intervals. Data are published quarterly under an open licence.

## Findings for 2023

Net shoreline retreat across the monitored coast averaged 1.8 metres in 2023, compared with 1.1 metres in 2022 and a ten-year mean of 0.9 metres. The acceleration is attributed primarily to two winter storm systems, designated Storm Bettina and Storm Cyrus, which made landfall in November 2023 within eleven days of each other.

Storm Bettina produced a peak significant wave height of 9.4 metres at the Ravensmouth buoy on 12 November 2023, the highest value recorded since the buoy was installed in 1997. Storm Cyrus followed on 23 November, generating a storm surge of 2.3 metres above predicted tide level at Sandhaven.

"The two storms together removed more sediment from this coast than the preceding four years combined," said Dr. Okonjo in the programme's December briefing. "What concerns us is that the beaches did not recover over the following winter, which is the pattern we would normally expect."

Twelve of the 74 stations recorded net accretion rather than erosion. Eleven of those twelve lie within 4 kilometres of an artificial headland or breakwater, which the report interprets as evidence that hard defences redistribute sediment locally rather than adding to the system.

## Sediment budget

The programme estimates that 1.24 million cubic metres of sand left the monitored cell in 2023. Of this, approximately 62% was transported offshore beyond the closure depth and is considered permanently lost to the beach system. The remaining 38% moved alongshore, largely southward, consistent with the dominant wave direction from the north-east.

Sediment input from the three rivers discharging into the cell totalled an estimated 190,000 cubic metres, well below the twenty-year average of 310,000 cubic metres. The report links the reduction to sediment retention behind the Kelbrook Reservoir, completed in 2019, which now traps material from a catchment of 880 square kilometres.

## Dune systems

Dune vegetation cover was surveyed at 22 sites. Mean cover fell from 71% in 2022 to 64% in 2023. Marram grass, the dominant stabilising species, declined at 18 of the 22 sites. Where cover fell below 50%, dune crest retreat averaged 3.4 metres, roughly four times the retreat measured at vegetated sites.

A trial planting of 40,000 marram culms was carried out at Thornwick Bay in March 2023. Survival after one growing season was 58%, which the report describes as within the expected range for exposed north-facing slopes.

## Instrumentation and data quality

Three monitoring stations were lost during Storm Bettina: stations 31, 44 and 52. Station 44 was recovered and redeployed in February 2024; the other two were destroyed. Replacement cost is estimated at £46,000 per station.

Data completeness for the year was 94.1%, down from 97.6% in 2022. The report attributes the shortfall to the station losses and to a firmware fault that affected fourteen wave sensors between June and August 2023. The affected records have been flagged rather than deleted.

The programme adopted a new grain-size analysis protocol in January 2023, replacing sieve analysis with laser diffraction. A cross-calibration exercise on 200 archived samples found that laser diffraction reports median grain size approximately 8% finer than sieving for well-sorted sands, and the historical series has been adjusted accordingly.

## Policy engagement

The programme submitted evidence to the parliamentary Coastal Resilience Inquiry in May 2023. Its central recommendation was that shoreline management plans be revised on a ten-year cycle rather than the current twenty-year cycle, on the grounds that the observed rate of change now exceeds the assumptions embedded in the existing plans.

The Department of Infrastructure has not yet responded to the recommendation. A spokesperson said in October 2023 that the department was "considering the evidence alongside other submissions."

Two local authorities, Ravensmouth Borough Council and North Sandhaven District Council, cited programme data in planning decisions during 2023. In both cases the data supported refusal of residential development within 60 metres of the dune toe.

## Outlook for 2024

The programme intends to install eight additional stations in 2024, concentrated on the southern third of the cell where station density is lowest. Funding for six of the eight has been confirmed.

A pilot study using uncrewed aerial survey will run at four sites between April and September 2024, with the aim of establishing whether monthly drone photogrammetry can substitute for some fortnightly ground surveys. If the accuracy target of ±0.05 metres vertical is met, the programme expects to reduce field survey costs by approximately 20% from 2026.

Dr. Okonjo will step down as director in September 2024 at the end of her secondment. Recruitment for a successor opened in January 2024.
